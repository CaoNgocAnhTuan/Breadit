This chapter gives the results of the offline experiments E1 to E6 and E8, and of the cost benchmark E7. Chapter 3 describes the data, the protocol, the methods and the statistics, so this chapter only reports what was measured. Chapter 7 discusses what the results mean.

All quality numbers use NDCG@10 unless a table says otherwise. ``Gain'' always means the paired difference $H - \text{BL2}$ per user, averaged over users. BL2 is the production Explore score, the reference of H1.

\section{How the experiments were run}
\label{sec:results-setup}

\textbf{Datasets.} Five synthetic worlds of size M (\texttt{syn-W0-M}, \texttt{syn-W1-M}, and the three W2 cells \texttt{syn-W2L-M}, \texttt{syn-W2M-M}, \texttt{syn-W2H-M}) and the real dataset Digg 2009 (\texttt{digg2009}). H1 has two co-primary tests: \texttt{syn-W2M-M} (the middle W2 cell) and \texttt{digg2009}.

\textbf{Order of work.} First, every tuned parameter was chosen on the validation items only, all before the cut time $T$. Second, the choices were frozen into the test configs, and the author approved them. Third, the test split was run once. No test result was seen before the tuning was frozen.

\textbf{Two changes before the test split.} Both were decided from validation results only, and both are recorded in the super-plan change log (version 0.25):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The H0 gate now tests H-follow, not the full $H$.} On W0, the full hybrid beat BL2 on validation, almost only through the community term. In the generator a community is a topic, so this term carries topic interest even when there is no social influence. H-follow ($\beta = 0$) has only the follow terms, and follows are random in W0, so it is the right null test.
  \item \textbf{The grids were widened.} The best $\alpha$ and $\beta$ were at the top of the first grid. The $\alpha$ and $\beta$ grid was extended, and the baseline grids were widened past their edges (Chapter 3 gives the final grids).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Statistics.} For \texttt{syn-W2M-M} and \texttt{digg2009}, family F1 holds the H1 comparisons ($H - \text{BL2}$ for each $K$ and history group) and family F2 holds $H$ against every other baseline. Each family is corrected with Holm on its own. All other comparisons get a bootstrap confidence interval and no $p$-value. H1 passes on a dataset only if, for NDCG@10 in group 0--4, the Holm-adjusted $p$ is below the Holm level \textbf{and} the lower end of the 95\% confidence interval is above 0.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Every offline run of this chapter (all but E7) was made a second time from empty folders, and every result file was byte-identical. The command \texttt{npm run thesis:repro} rebuilds all of them from the raw data and compares each file with the merged manifest \texttt{.docs/results/manifest.json}. It passed on the development machine and on a clean clone of the repository. The E7 timings are repeatable, not identical (Section~\ref{sec:results-e7}).
