Bibliographic check: yes. Title, authors, year (2019) and venue (RecSys 2019, ACM Reference Format on page 1) match the request. Only a case difference in the title ("Are we really..." vs "Are We Really..."), which is not a real difference.

Says:
The authors checked recent neural methods for top-n recommendation. These came from top conferences between 2015 and 2018. They could rerun only 7 of the 18 methods with reasonable effort. For 6 of those 7, simple well-tuned methods often did as well or better. The 7th beat the simple baselines but not a well-tuned linear method in every case.

Answers:

Q1. Tried to reproduce: 18 papers. Reproduced: 7.
- 18: Section 1, page 2 (line 140): "We identified 18 relevant papers."
- 7: Section 1, page 2 (line 145): "an acceptable degree of certainty for only 7 papers."

Q2. Baselines: TopPopular, UserKNN, ItemKNN, ItemKNN-CBF, ItemKNN-CFCBF, P3 alpha, RP3 beta (Section 2.2, page 3). Tuning: Bayesian search (scikit-optimize) for all baselines and datasets, with 35 cases per algorithm, the first 5 being random starts (Section 2.2, page 4). Ranges given: neighborhood size k from 5 to 800, shrink term h from 0 to 1000, alpha and beta from 0 to 2.
- Quote (page 4, line 334): "We explored 35 cases for each algorithm"

Q3. Main finding (Section 1, page 2): in 6 of the 7 reproduced cases the neural methods did not consistently beat the tuned simple baselines. Quote:
- "not consistently outperform the simple, but fine-tuned, baseline" (page 2, line 155)

Q4. Evaluation protocol: no single protocol for the whole study. Each reproduced method was run with its own original protocol (Section 2.2, page 3). The paper says the work reproduces each original evaluation procedure, and the baselines were run with the same code.
- Quote (page 3, line 250): "the original implementations in a way that allows us to apply the"
- Per paper (for reference): CMN leave-one-out, HR and NDCG at 5 and 10, Section 3.1; MCRec 80/20 random splits repeated 10 times, Precision, Recall, NDCG at 10, Section 3.2; CVAE random splits repeated 5 times, Recall at 50, 100, 300, Section 3.3; NCF leave-one-out, HR and NDCG up to 10, Section 3.5; SpectralCF 80/20 random splits, Recall and MAP at several cutoffs, Section 3.6; Mult-VAE Recall at 20 and 50 and NDCG at 100, Section 3.7. Quote for the random 80/20 setting (page 6, line 597): "The evaluation was based on randomly created 80/20 training-"

Q5. Learned methods and reproduction (Section 2.1, page 2): the paper says that even with the technical description, many details are missing and affect results.
- Quote (page 2, line 188): "many tiny details regarding the implementation of the algorithms"

Not found:
- One common data split, metric set or cutoff used for all 18 papers. The paper says these vary (Section 4.2, page 8).
- Names of the 18 papers in the text. Table 1 (page 3) gives only reference numbers.
- Per-year reproducibility numbers. The paper says they are "not shown here for space reasons" (page 3, Section 2.1).
- Re-run of hyper-parameter tuning for the reproduced neural methods. Footnote 4 (page 3) says this is future work.
