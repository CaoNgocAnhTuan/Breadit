Says:
Ranking measures usually assume that no two results have the same score. When scores are tied, the order of the tied results can change the measure value. The simple fix of picking one valid order can give different results for each pick. The authors adapt six measures so they give the average over all orders, but without making the orders. The method is deterministic and about as fast as the normal measures.

Answers:

Q1. Problem with ties.
Section 1 (Introduction), p. 414: "Typically these performance measures assume that a ranking algorithm arranges the results of a query into a total ordering"
Section 1, p. 415: "different well-sorted permutations of result vectors can have" (the next words in the text are "appreciably different performance values"; the sentence is about tie orders changing the result)

Q2. What the authors propose, and which metrics.
Section 2 intro, p. 416: "rather than explicitly producing these orderings we analytically derive their average"
Section 1, p. 415: "It is completely deterministic"
Metrics, Abstract, p. 414: "we show how to adapt six popular measures"
The six metrics are precision (2.1), recall (2.2), F1 (2.3), average precision (2.4), reciprocal rank (2.5) and NDCG (2.6). The tie handling is the average over all possible tie orders, computed in closed form. It is not random sampling and not a fixed tie-break rule.

Q3. Can values change with arbitrary tie-breaking?
Section 1, p. 415: "large enough to affect the relative ordering"
The authors say that different well-sorted permutations can have appreciably different performance values. This is an observation from their own large web test sets; they give no formula. They do not give a numeric example in the paper. Item-id tie-breaking is not discussed.

Q4. Efficiency and cost.
Section 1, p. 415: "its time complexity is super-exponential to the number of tied results"
Section 2.4, p. 419: "the time complexity to compute AP@k(V ) is O(k) in the tie-oblivious case"
Section 3, p. 421: "overhead is roughly 25%"
Section 4, p. 421: "not appreciably slower than computing the standard, tie-oblivious performance measures"
Cost: the tie-aware versions are about as fast as the normal ones. Reciprocal rank has about 25% overhead in their test; for the other measures the overhead is "negligible" according to Section 3, p. 421.

Q5. Recommendation for practice.
Not found as an explicit recommendation. The closest statements are: Section 1, p. 415: "Our approach is arguably superior to the two aforementioned approaches"
Section 3, p. 416: "are routinely using them as cost functions"
These show what the authors do, not what others should do.
