Bibliographic check: Title matches. Year 2009 and venue WWW 2009 (18th International World Wide Web Conference, Madrid, pages 681-690) match. Authors differ. The real author list is Wen-Yen Chen, Jon-Chyuan Chu, Junyi Luan, Hongjie Bai, Yi Wang and Edward Y. Chang (six authors, not Chen, Chen, Zhang, Zhang et al.). The DOI 10.1145/1526709.1526801 is confirmed by Crossref. The title in Crossref has no subtitle, but the PDF has it.

Says: The paper compares two ways to recommend online communities on Orkut. The first is association rule mining, which finds communities that many users join together. The second is LDA, a topic model that finds hidden groups of communities. The authors test both on a large Orkut data set and count how often the left-out community is ranked high. LDA does better for lists of four or more communities, and the rule method does a bit better for shorter lists.

Answers:
Q1. Task: Recommending communities to users, not posts. Section 3.1, PDF p. 3 (printed p. 683): "we can recommend communities to a user based on his joined communities"

Q2. Algorithms: ARM, using FP-growth to mine frequent itemsets (Section 3.1), and LDA, trained with Gibbs sampling (Section 3.2). Membership use: ARM treats each user as a basket of communities. Section 3.1, PDF p. 3 (printed p. 683): "we view each user as a transaction and his joined communities as items"
LDA uses the same membership as 1 or 0 counts. Section 3.2, PDF p. 3 (printed p. 683): "user-community data is entered as a membership count where the value is 1 (join) or 0 (not join)"

Q3. Data: 492,104 users and 118,002 communities, English communities only, from July 2007 (Section 5.1.1, PDF p. 5, printed p. 685). The matrix density is 0.01286%. Evaluation: top-k recommendation metric (Section 5.1.2, PDF p. 5, printed p. 685). One joined community per user is held out, and 1000 random non-joined communities are added (k = 1001). Section 5.1.2, PDF p. 5, printed p. 685: "we randomly withhold one joined community c from his original set of joined communities"

Q4. Findings: The abstract says LDA is better for 4 or more communities, and ARM is slightly better for up to 3 communities. Abstract, PDF p. 1 (printed p. 681): "LDA performs consistently better than ARM for the community recommendation task when recommending a list of 4 or more communities" Other results: Figure 3 (cumulative rank curves, PDF p. 6, printed p. 685) and Figure 4 (top 2%, PDF p. 6, printed p. 686). Figure 5 shows most held-out communities rank better with LDA. Section 5.1.3, PDF p. 7, printed p. 686: "Most of the withheld communities have positive rank difference rather than negative rank difference." Scalability (Table 9, Figure 7, Section 5.2, PDF p. 9, printed p. 688): "approximately linear speedup on up to 8 machines"

Q5. Friend or social-tie data: Not found. The method uses only membership data. The only social mention is a general description of Orkut as a social network service (Section 5.1.1, PDF p. 5, printed p. 685). Section 6, PDF p. 10, printed p. 690, says future work is to use counts such as forum posts and visits, not social ties: "our current user-community data is binary-valued to denote membership or non-membership (one or zero)"

Not found:
- Any friend, trust or social-tie data in the method, data or experiments (searched for: friend, social tie, tie, trust, network, contact). Only the Orkut description and the word "friendz" in a community name appear.
- Exact values of the ARM and LDA top-k percentages at specific list sizes in text form. Results are only shown in Figures 3 and 4 and were not read off the plots.
- Table 9 text is garbled in the PDF conversion. The speedup values (1.93 to 10.66) are in the text, but the total times for each machine count are not clearly readable. The conclusion says training falls "from 8 hours to less than 46 minutes" with 32 machines (Section 6, PDF p. 10, printed p. 690), so I did not use any other total time.
