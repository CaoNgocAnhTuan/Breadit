Says:
This short paper argues that recommender systems should not be judged on accuracy alone. It looks at two other measures, coverage and serendipity. It reviews how earlier work measured them and how they trade off against each other. It proposes new ways to measure both, and adds a usefulness value to each. It runs no experiments; it only describes a planned user study as future work.

Answers:
Q1. Coverage. Section 2 (PDF p. 2). The paper uses the definition from Herlocker et al. and names two kinds: prediction coverage and catalog coverage. Catalog coverage is the share of available items that the system ever recommends. It is measured over a set of recommendation sessions, with N recommendations in total. The paper also proposes a weighted version that counts only useful items. Quote (PDF p. 2): "the percentage of the available items which effectively are ever recommended to a user". The formula text is partly broken in the text layer, so this summary follows the words around it.
Q2. Serendipity. Section 3 (PDF pp. 2 to 3). The paper starts from the Herlocker et al. definition. Quote (PDF p. 3): "in [4] serendipity is defined as a measure of the extent to which the recommended items are both attractive and surprising to the users". The paper's own metric has two steps. First, an item is unexpected if the recommender lists it and a simple primitive prediction model does not. Second, the metric averages a usefulness value (1 or 0, judged by the user) over the unexpected items. Quote (PDF p. 3): "this new serendipity metric not only determines the ratio".
Q3. Main argument. Section 1 (PDF p. 1). Quote: "few recent works argue that accuracy is not the only metric for". The paper's own main claim is in the abstract: the new measures should match the quality the user perceives better than older metrics. This is stated in the abstract, not as a quote.
Q4. Data, algorithms and evaluation setup. None were used. The paper names no dataset and tests no algorithm. The only concrete setup is a planned user study with song recommendations, where users hear a 30-second clip and answer whether they knew the song and whether they liked it. Quote (PDF p. 3): "We present a list of song recommendations to a user who can listen to". Real experiments are stated as future work (PDF p. 4): "Experiments with the usage of these metrics in real scenarios are".
Q5. Main findings. The paper has no numbers, tables or figures, so there are no results to report. Its finding is a qualitative trade-off. Section 4 (PDF p. 4, journal p. 260) says accuracy and coverage pull against each other, citing Good et al. 1999. Quote: "coverage usually decreases as a function of accuracy". It also says more serendipity needs more catalog coverage, and more focus on accuracy means less serendipity. Quote: "the less serendipity happens".
