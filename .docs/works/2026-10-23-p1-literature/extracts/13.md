Bibliographic check: yes. Title, authors (Zaiqiao Meng, Richard McCreadie, Craig Macdonald, Iadh Ounis), year 2020 and venue (RecSys '20, 14th ACM Recommender Systems Conference) match the paper. The arXiv copy I read is v1. I did not check whether a later arXiv version exists.

Says:
The way a dataset is split into training and test parts changes how recommender models rank against each other. The authors looked at 17 recent papers and found many different split styles. They tested seven models on two grocery datasets with three split types. Leave-one-last splits can let future data leak into training, so the authors recommend the global temporal split as the default. Their main finding is that the split is a confounding variable, so results from papers with different splits are hard to compare.

Answers:

Q1. Strategies compared and time order.
- Leave one last item (per user, last item for test). Section 2, p. 2 to 3.
- Leave one last basket/session (per user, last basket for test). Section 2, p. 3.
- Temporal user split (last percentage of each user's history for test). Section 2, p. 3. Not compared in the experiments.
- Temporal global split (one time point shared by all users). Section 2, p. 3. Compared.
- Random split (random boundary per user). Section 2, p. 4. Not compared.
- User split (users, not interactions, go to train or test). Section 2, p. 4. Not compared.
- Section 3 compares three: leave one last item, leave one last basket and global temporal split (p. 4).
- Time order: only temporal global clearly uses a shared global timeline. Temporal user "does consider the global interaction timestamps" (p. 3), but its boundary differs per user. Leave one last takes each user's last transaction, and the paper does not describe it as a global timeline. Random and user split do not use time order.
- Quote (Section 3, p. 4): "we omit the user split scheme since it is both rarely used and mandates a very different evaluation pipeline"

Q2. Recommendation or conclusion on leave-one-last, random split and time-based split.
- Leave one last: the paper says it maximizes the training data, but it can leak future evidence into training and it is the most popular strategy in the 17 papers (8 of 17). Section 2, p. 3.
- Random split: the paper says it "has been gradually abandoned" for leave one last, and its splits are not reproducible unless the authors release them. Section 2, p. 4.
- Time-based: temporal global is called the most realistic setting, and the paper recommends it as the default. Section 5, p. 7.
- Quote (Section 5, p. 7, best practice 2): "This is generally seen as the most realistic setting, and so should be the default splitting strategy used."

Q3. Size of the split effect.
- Under the same dataset and metric, the ranking of the 7 models changes between splits. Example: for Dunnhumby with Recall@10, the worst model under leave one last item (NMF) moves up three places under leave one last basket (Table 3, p. 6).
- Swaps happen for all pairs of splits, and they cluster around the best models (Section 4, p. 6).
- With 230 NeuMF, VBCAR and Triple2Vec models (Figure 2, p. 7), Kendall's tau between split pairs is moderate.
- Quote (Section 4, p. 6): "the Kendall’s τ correlations between the pairs of splitting strategies are only moderate, ranging between 0.5284 and 0.7630"
- I did not use the numeric cells of Table 3 because the text extraction of that table is garbled.

Q4. Datasets, models, metrics and cut-offs.
- Datasets: Tafeng and Dunnhumby, both grocery transaction data (Section 3, p. 5, Table 2).
- Models: NMF, BPR, VAECF, NeuMF, NGCF, Triple2vec and VBCAR (Section 3, p. 5). The paper also says NeuMF, Triple2vec and VBCAR were tuned for the second experiment (p. 5).
- Metrics: NDCG@10 and Recall@10, plus Kendall's tau for ranking correlation (Section 3, p. 5).
- Quote (Section 3, p. 5): "we experiment with a set of seven recommenders from the literature"

Q5. Sampled versus full ranking, ties, and leakage.
- Sampled versus full ranking: not found. The paper does not say how candidate items are ranked. Table 2 says "post sampling" (p. 5), but it refers to filtering of users and items, not to an evaluation protocol.
- Ties: not found.
- Data leakage: yes. The paper says leave one last and temporal user splits leak future interactions into training. Section 2, p. 3.
- Quote (Section 2, p. 3): "resulting in evidence about future interactions ‘leaking’ into the model during training"
