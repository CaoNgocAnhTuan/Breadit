Bibliographic check: Title matches. Year 2019 and venue SIGIR 2019 match (page header: SIGIR'19). Author list differs. The paper lists Le Wu, Peijie Sun, Yanjie Fu, Richang Hong, Xiting Wang and Meng Wang. Our request said 'Fan', but the paper has 'Fu'. Our request also said 'Tang', but Tang is not an author of this paper. Correct form: Wu, Sun, Fu, Hong, Wang, Wang.

Says:
DiffNet is a neural model for social recommendation. It spreads each user's embedding through the social network in steps. At each step, a user's embedding is mixed with the average embedding of the users she trusts. The paper uses K = 2 steps. The abstract claims more than 13 percent gain over the best baselines for top-10 recommendation.

Answers:

Q1. Hop and layer definition, number of layers.
- Definition: Section 3.1 (Influence Diffusion Layers), page 4. The paper does not use the word 'hop' in its definition. Each layer takes the previous layer's user embeddings and averages them over the trusted users (Eq. 9, page 4). Quote: "This influence diffusion step is repeated for K steps to" (Section 3.1, page 4).
- Link to hops: Section 4.4, page 8. The authors give a hypothesis, not a proof. Quote: "considering the K-step recursive social diffusion process resembles" (Section 4.4, page 8).
- K in the model: K is a predefined depth (Section 3.1, page 4). Best value is K = 2 (Section 3.3.1, page 5). Quote: "DiffNet reaches the best performance when K= 2." (Section 3.3.1, page 5).
- K in the experiments: K = 2 (Section 4.1, page 6, stated in a sentence that the text extraction splits over two lines, so no quote).

Q2. Effect of more layers.
- Yes. Table 4 (Section 4.4, page 8) gives HR@10 and NDCG@10 for K = 0 to 3.
- Yelp HR@10: K=0 0.3145 (-9.54%), K=1 0.3390 (-2.50%), K=2 0.3477 (best), K=3 0.3348 (-3.72%).
- Flickr HR@10: K=0 0.1439 (-12.27%), K=1 0.1592 (-2.96%), K=2 0.1641 (best), K=3 0.1603 (-2.34%).
- NDCG@10: Yelp K=0 0.2014, K=3 0.2005; Flickr K=0 0.1148, K=1 0.1257, K=3 0.1246. The printed Yelp K=1 NDCG value (0.2981) does not match its stated -1.93% change; the value should be near 0.208. This looks like a typo.
- Trend: performance rises from K=0 to K=2, then drops at K=3. Quote: "to 3, the performance drops for both datasets." (Section 4.4, page 8).

Q3. Datasets, task, metrics.
- Yelp: location-based social network, ratings above 3 count as likes (Section 4.1, page 6). Quote: "larger than 3 as the liked items by this user." Yelp counts from Table 1 (page 6): 17,237 users, 38,342 items, 143,765 social links, 204,448 ratings.
- Flickr: image sharing site, upvotes count as likes. Quote: "Users follow other users and share their preferences to" (Section 4.1, page 6). Flickr counts from Table 1 (page 6): 8,358 users, 82,120 items, 187,273 social links, 314,809 ratings.
- Table 1 text is jumbled in the PDF text. I matched the numbers to the rows by checking the density values (0.048%, 0.031%, 0.268%, 0.046%) against the counts.
- Task: top-N recommendation with implicit feedback. Quote: "As we focus on recommending top-N items for each user, we" (Section 4.1, page 6).
- Metrics: Hit Ratio (HR) and NDCG at N = 5, 10, 15 (Tables 2 and 3, page 7). Quote: "use two widely adopted ranking based metrics: Hit Ratio (HR) and" (Section 4.1, page 6).
- Split: 10% test; 10% of the remaining training data for validation (Section 4.1, page 6, no quote).

Q4. Baselines.
- Compared: BPR, FM, TrustSVD, ContextMF, GC-MC and PinSage (Section 4.1, page 6). SVD++ appears in Tables 2 and 3 (page 7) but is not in the Section 4.1 list. Quote: "various state-of-the-art baselines, including the classical pair-wise" (Section 4.1, page 6).
- Simple non-neural baseline: yes. BPR is a latent factor method. Quote: "factor based technique for dealing with implicit feedback." (Section 5, page 9). FM, SVD++ and TrustSVD are also latent factor models. The paper does not call them neural, so this label is my reading.
- Graph convolution baselines: GC-MC and PinSage (Section 4.1, page 6).
- NeuMF is discussed in related work only and is not a baseline.

Q5. Social graph and users with few interactions.
- Type: a user-user graph S with links from user a to user b. Quote: "as a user-user directed graph" (Section 2.1, page 2). The paper also allows an undirected graph. Quote: "the social network is undirected, then user a connects to user b" (Section 2.1, page 2).
- Flickr is described as a 'who-trust-whom' platform where users follow others (Section 4.1, page 6). Yelp users 'make friends' (Section 4.1, page 6). The paper does not say whether each dataset's links are directed or undirected.
- Filtering: users with fewer than 2 rating records and fewer than 2 social links were removed (Section 4.1, page 6). Quote: "out users that have less than 2 rating records and 2 social links," (Section 4.1, page 6).
- Sparsity analysis: users are grouped by number of training ratings (Section 4.3, page 7). Quote: "rating records of each user is less than 16, the BPR baseline could" (Section 4.3, page 7).

Not found:
- A formal definition of 'hop'. The only link to k-hop neighbours is a hypothesis in Section 4.4, page 8.
- Results for K above 3, and any result for K = 4 or more.
- Results for layers on sparse groups. Figure 2 (page 8) is a chart. I read the text layer only and did not check the figure values.
- Whether the Yelp and Flickr social links are directed or undirected in the data.
- Any result or rule for users with zero ratings or zero social links, including how the average pooling in Eq. 9 handles a user with no trusted users. No cold-start test is reported.
- SVD++ in the Section 4.1 baseline list (it appears only in Tables 2 and 3).
- Table reference error: Section 4.4 text (page 8) says 'Table 4 shows the performance on DiffNet with different fusion inputs', but that is Table 5.
- Possible typo in Table 2: GC-MC Flickr value '0.9450' seems to be 0.0945 (the other values in that row are near 0.09).
